Lesson One

Embracing the "P" Word

Introduction

There is a "P" word floating around out there, that no one wants to talk about. It's not pornography, it's purity.

The word pure gets a bad rap, especially in masculine circles. Men tend to look at this word and assign weakness to it — something frail, callow, naïve, soft, sheltered, or untested.

In reality, pure by definition speaks to strength, resiliency, wholeness, and something that is clearly and unmistakably defined, or refined. It speaks to something measured to a clear standard — something that has a clear meaning and purpose.

Something that is pure is undivided, uncontaminated, and is unmistakable.

The Problem
We have a .

There are signs that exist that tell us we have a purity problem in our culture today, especially as it relates to men. More than any other time in history there are indications that men are struggling with a clear and defined sense of purpose, wholeness, standard, strength, and resolve.

  • Growing number of fatherless homes
  • Becoming more common for men to be unhappy or struggling in their marriages
  • Fractured families (divorce, split custody)
  • Proliferation of lust, affairs, pornography
  • Instability and low self-worth (suicide and depression)
  • Lack of direction and purpose
  • Degradation of standards and character (cheating, shortcuts, gambling)
  • Overly materialistic & self-centered ambitions
  • Increase in substance abuse / reliance
  • Growing sense of entitlement and a weakening of work-ethic
Impurity's Reach
all of us.

Impurities that attack any one of us are common to all of us (1 Corinthians 10:13). They try to find their way into any and every part of our lives seeking to dilute and pollute our hearts and minds.

Dilution occurs when there is a mixture of something pure with something lesser. When this occurs, strength, potency, and clarity weaken.

Genesis 3:1–4 (NIV)

Now the serpent was more crafty than any of the wild animals the Lord God had made. He said to the woman, "Did God really say, 'You must not eat from any tree in the garden'?" The woman said to the serpent, "We may eat fruit from the trees in the garden, but God did say, 'You must not eat fruit from the tree that is in the middle of the garden, and you must not touch it, or you will die.'" "You will not certainly die," the serpent said to the woman.

Pollutants corrupt and contaminate. They introduce foreign or harmful elements into an otherwise stable or pure environment.

1 Corinthians 5:6 (NLT)

Your boasting about this is terrible. Don't you realize that this sin is like a little yeast that spreads through the whole batch of dough?

James 1:27 (CEB)

True devotion, the kind that is pure and faultless before God the Father, is this: to care for orphans and widows in their difficulties and to keep the world from contaminating us.

The Solution
Purity problems are best resolved .

You can seek to solve a problem, but not resolve the issue. You'll never fully solve a problem unless you get to the source of the problem.

James 3:11–12 (NIV)

Can both fresh water and salt water flow from the same spring? My brothers and sisters, can a fig tree bear olives, or a grapevine bear figs? Neither can a salt spring produce fresh water.

David, the man described as one who had a heart patterned after God's (1 Samuel 13:14, Acts 13:22) found himself lured into an affair with another man's wife (2 Samuel 11).

After being confronted by the prophet Nathan, David repented to the Lord, asking the Lord to deal permanently with the sins of his own desires. David had to go to the source of his sin.
