Lesson Four

A Better Way Forward

Stripping Off the Weight

The Bible tells us that each of us accumulate things over the course of our lives that weigh us down and prevent us from moving forward into the life that God has called us to live.

Hebrews 12:1 (NLT)

Therefore, since we are surrounded by such a huge crowd of witnesses to the life of faith, let us strip off every weight that slows us down, especially the sin that so easily trips us up. And let us run with endurance the race God has set before us.

If we are going to be pure, whole, and strong men we are going to have to deal with the parts of our past that are acting as weight or hindrances to God's best for our lives.

Our past can have a major impact on our future if we let it. We have to reconcile the reality that our past is a part of our life, but yet at the same time, it doesn't have to negatively impact our future.

Philippians 3:14 (TLB)

I don't mean to say that I have already achieved these things or that I have already reached perfection. But I press on to possess that perfection for which Christ Jesus first possessed me. No, dear brothers, I am still not all I should be, but I am bringing all my energies to bear on this one thing: Forgetting the past and looking forward to what lies ahead, I strain to reach the end of the race and receive the prize for which God is calling us up to heaven because of what Christ Jesus did for us.

Let's look at some lessons that we can learn about our past.

Our Past Shows Up
Our past in our .

We all have a past and our past has a way of making its way into our present.

Psalm 51:3 (NIV)

For I know my transgressions, and my sin is always before me.

Psalm 38:4 (NIV)

My guilt has overwhelmed me like a burden too heavy to bear.

The weights of our past typically show up because:
  • We live with shame and regret
  • We replay scenarios and circumstances
  • We have allowed words from others to shape how we see ourselves
  • We harbor bitterness, offenses, and resentments toward others
  • We nurture wounds
  • We live with a void from not feeling fully loved and accepted
  • We fear that one day our past will repeat itself or catch up to us
Deal with the Past
with the

We will never experience life to the fullest unless we face our past head on.

Symptoms of an undealt-with past
  • Depression
  • Low self-worth
  • Always feel behind or left out
  • Consistently trying to prove yourself or please people
  • Performance anxiety
  • Perfectionism
  • Repetitive cycles of sin
  • Resisting emotions
  • Uncontrolled emotions
  • Reciprocate behaviors
  • Detachment
  • Isolation
  • Insulation
  • Workaholic
  • Masking / Coping
Appropriately dealing with our past means seeking to loosen the destructive grip and lessen the negative influence that our past has on our lives. It implies purposefully identifying the chains keeping us bound and stepping out of the cycles that keep us stuck.

Reflection Questions
Question 1
What are the specific "weights" from your past that are still affecting you today (are they sins, wounds, words spoken over you, or something else…)?
Finish this statement: "The weight I'm still carrying is _________."
Question 2
Where do you see your past influencing your current decisions, reactions, or relationships?
Question 3
Review the list of symptoms listed — which 2–3 show up most in your life right now? What might these symptoms be pointing back to?
Question 4
Where have you been avoiding dealing with your past? What have you minimized, buried, or just tried to "move on" from?
Question 5
When it comes to your past, have you truly brought it to God, or just tried to manage it yourself? What would it look like to genuinely surrender that area to Him this week?
Question 6
Is there anything from your past that requires ownership or making it right? Where do you need to take responsibility instead of just feeling regret?
Question 7
Who do you still need to forgive? Or—just as important—what are you still holding against yourself?
Question 8
Where have you started to believe "This is just how it's always going to be"? What would it look like to actually believe that God can redeem and restore your past?
Question 9
Where are you trying to handle things on your own that you shouldn't be? Who is one person you can invite into your process (mentor, counselor, trusted friend)?
Question 10
What is one new habit you need to build to move forward? What is one old pattern you need to intentionally replace?
Question 11
How could God use your past to help someone else? What part of your story are you still hesitant to share—and why?
Question 12
What is one step you will take this week to loosen the grip of your past and move forward?
